
Back in school, when I was really into industrial design, I watched a [video of Jony Ive](https://youtu.be/4xzLr7xSr-g) walking through Apple's hardware and software. Compared to Apple's slick ads today, it was a pretty plain video. But I wanted to understand the process and the thinking behind the design, so I was hooked. His seriousness and the way he thought about design stuck with me for a long time.

Recently I stumbled on his [commencement speech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gr3GKcwG9s8&pp=ugMICgJrbxABGAHKBQxqb255IGl2ZSBjY2E%3D) by chance. He hadn't changed a bit. But this time it wasn't about the products. It was more personal. Lately I've been buried in metrics and routine at work, and it brought back something I'd forgotten a long time ago.

Here are a few lines that stuck with me.

.

> I loved my practice, and it started to help inform my sense of identity.
>
> <br>
>
> It helped me understand how I could contribute to society and to culture.
>
> <br>
>
> I began to think of myself as a designer rather than a shy person that couldn't read very well or speak with any confidence.   
>

> As I became more preoccupied though with becoming a professional designer, I started to worry about an imbalance.
>
> <br>
>
> While developing my design practice legitimized and channeled my creativity. I started to feel that my childhood dreaming and imagination was being left behind.
>
> <br>
>
> Now colleges is of course exactly their place, exactly their time to focus on developing our professional skills.
>
> <br>
>
> But, what bothered me was just how easily my attention was consumed with the specific skills of design rather than the fundamentally important ones of creating.
>
> <br>
>
> Not to sound hopelessly dramatic, but I almost felt I was betraying the dreaming and the wondering that had really sustained me as a child.
>
> <br>
>
> Without imagination, without profoundly new thinking and potent ideas our practice has no purpose.

> I came to learn from him(Roy Morris) that creating and thinking should always be afforded a rare respect.
>
> <br>
>
> This is the important thing, always a rare respect, not only when the ideas are good and not only the circumstances are easy and convenient.

> Lastly one of the wonderful consequences of being open is that you find yourself actually listening.
>
> <br>
>
> Listening well means you need to be quiet.
>
> <br>
>
> Great ideas can come from the quietest voice.
